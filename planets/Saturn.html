Saturn

Lord of the rings — a golden gas giant with moons of ice and mystery.

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second largest in the Solar System. It is best known for its spectacular ring system — thousands of ringlets made of ice and rock particles ranging from dust to house-sized chunks. Saturn is less dense than water; if a bathtub big enough existed, the planet would float. Its many moons include two of the most intriguing worlds in the Solar System: Titan and Enceladus.

Key Facts

  • Saturn's rings extend up to 282,000 km from the planet but are only about 10 meters thick in places.
  • The rings are mostly water ice with a smaller fraction of rocky material and organic compounds.
  • A permanent hexagonal jet stream swirls around Saturn's north pole — a unique atmospheric feature.
  • Cassini orbited Saturn for 13 years (2004–2017), revolutionizing our understanding of the system.
  • Saturn radiates 2.5 times more energy than it receives from the Sun, powered by internal heat.
  • Its low density (0.687 g/cm³) makes it the least dense planet — it would float in water.
  • Seasons on Saturn last about 7 Earth years each due to its 29.5-year orbital period.
  • Lightning storms on Saturn can be a thousand times more powerful than those on Earth.
  • The rings may be relatively young — perhaps only 100 million years old — or constantly recycled.
  • Saturn's moon Titan is the only moon with a thick atmosphere and stable liquids on its surface.

Major Moons

Titan

Diameter: 5,150 km Orbit period: 15.95 days
  • Titan is Saturn's largest moon and the second-largest moon in the Solar System.
  • Its thick nitrogen atmosphere (1.5× Earth's pressure) shrouds the surface from direct view.
  • Lakes and seas of liquid methane and ethane exist near the poles — the only stable surface liquids beyond Earth.
  • The Huygens probe landed on Titan in 2005, sending back images of river channels and rounded icy pebbles.
  • Titan's dense haze makes it a laboratory for studying prebiotic chemistry.

Enceladus

Diameter: 504 km Orbit period: 1.37 days
  • Enceladus shoots geysers of water vapor and ice from cracks at its south pole into space.
  • Cassini flew through these plumes and detected organic molecules, salts, and hydrogen.
  • A global subsurface ocean lies beneath its icy crust, heated by tidal flexing from Saturn.
  • Its bright, reflective surface makes Enceladus one of the most reflective bodies in the Solar System.
  • It is a leading candidate for hosting microbial life in its warm, salty ocean.
