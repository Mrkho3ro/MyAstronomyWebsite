Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only astronomical object known to harbor life. Liquid water covers about 71% of its surface, a protective magnetic field shields the atmosphere from solar wind, and plate tectonics continuously recycle the crust. From orbit, Earth appears as a fragile blue marble — a reminder of how rare and precious habitable worlds may be.

Key Facts

  • Earth is the densest planet in the Solar System, with an iron-nickel core and silicate mantle.
  • The magnetic field, generated by the rotating core, deflects harmful charged particles from the Sun.
  • Plate tectonics create mountains, ocean trenches, and earthquakes while regulating long-term CO₂ levels.
  • Earth's axial tilt of 23.5° causes the seasons as the planet orbits the Sun.
  • About 71% of the surface is ocean; the Pacific Ocean alone covers more area than all land combined.
  • Earth's atmosphere blocks most harmful UV radiation and burns up small meteors before they reach the ground.
  • Life appeared at least 3.5 billion years ago; oxygen from photosynthesis transformed the atmosphere.
  • Earth has one natural satellite — the Moon — which stabilizes our axial tilt and drives ocean tides.
  • Human activity is now a major force shaping climate, ecosystems, and geology — the Anthropocene era.
  • From the Moon, astronauts saw Earth as a "pale blue dot" — a symbol of global unity and environmental stewardship.

Moons

The Moon

Diameter: 3,474 km Orbit period: 27.3 days Distance: ~384,400 km
  • The Moon is the fifth-largest moon in the Solar System and the largest relative to its parent planet.
  • It likely formed when a Mars-sized body collided with early Earth ~4.5 billion years ago.
  • The same side always faces Earth because the Moon is tidally locked in its orbit.
  • Lunar gravity drives ocean tides and has gradually slowed Earth's rotation over billions of years.
  • Apollo astronauts brought back 382 kg of rock, proving the Moon was once molten and geologically active.
