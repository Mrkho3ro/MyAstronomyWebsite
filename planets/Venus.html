Venus

Earth's twin in size — a furnace world shrouded in toxic clouds.

Venus is often called Earth's "sister planet" because of similar size and mass, but its environment could not be more different. A runaway greenhouse effect traps heat beneath thick sulfuric-acid clouds, making Venus the hottest planet in the Solar System — even hotter than Mercury despite being farther from the Sun.

Key Facts

  • Venus rotates backward (retrograde) — the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east.
  • A Venusian day (243 Earth days) is longer than its year (225 Earth days).
  • Surface pressure is about 92 bar — roughly 90 times Earth's, like being 900 m underwater.
  • Thick clouds reflect sunlight brilliantly, making Venus the brightest natural object in the night sky after the Moon.
  • Radar mapping by Magellan revealed vast volcanic plains, highlands, and thousands of volcanoes.
  • There is no liquid water on the surface today; any ancient oceans likely boiled away billions of years ago.
  • Super-rotating winds in the upper atmosphere circle the planet in about 4 Earth days.
  • Soviet Venera landers were the first to transmit images from another planet's surface (1970s–1980s).
  • Venus may still be geologically active, with evidence of recent volcanic resurfacing.
  • Studying Venus helps scientists understand how runaway greenhouse effects can transform habitable worlds.
