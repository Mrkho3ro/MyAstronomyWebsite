Neptune

The farthest planet — deep blue, windswept, and crowned by Triton.

Ice giant

At a Glance

  • Diameter49,244 km (equator)
  • Mass1.02 × 10²⁶ kg (17× Earth)
  • Surface gravity11.15 m/s² (1.14× Earth)*
  • Day length16 h 6 min
  • Year length165 Earth years
  • Atmosphere80% H₂, 19% He, 1.5% CH₄
  • Avg. cloud temp.~−200 °C
  • Known moons16 confirmed
  • Discovered1846 (predicted mathematically)

Neptune is the eighth and farthest known planet from the Sun. This deep-blue ice giant was the first planet located through mathematical prediction rather than direct observation — astronomers calculated where it must be to explain Uranus's orbital wobble. Neptune has the strongest sustained winds in the Solar System and a complex system of rings and moons, dominated by the captured world Triton.

Key Facts

  • Neptune was predicted by Urbain Le Verrier and John Couch Adams, then found by Johann Galle in 1846.
  • Winds on Neptune reach up to 2,100 km/h (1,300 mph) — the fastest in the Solar System.
  • The Great Dark Spot, a storm similar to Jupiter's Red Spot, was observed by Voyager 2 in 1989.
  • Neptune radiates 2.6 times more energy than it receives from the Sun — its interior is still cooling.
  • It has completed less than one full orbit since its discovery in 1846 (one year = 165 Earth years).
  • Neptune's deep blue color comes from methane absorbing red light in the upper atmosphere.
  • It has faint rings discovered in 1984, made of dust and ice particles darkened by radiation.
  • Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft to visit Neptune, flying by on August 25, 1989.
  • Neptune's magnetic field is tilted 47° from its rotation axis and offset from the planet's center.
  • At 30 AU from the Sun, Neptune receives about 1/900 the sunlight that Earth does.

Major Moons

Proteus

Diameter: 420 km Orbit period: 1.12 days
  • Proteus is Neptune's second-largest moon and the largest irregularly shaped moon that is in hydrostatic equilibrium.
  • Pharos crater dominates one hemisphere — one of the largest craters relative to body size known.
  • It orbits just outside Neptune's faint ring system.

Triton

Diameter: 2,706 km Orbit period: 5.88 days (retrograde)
  • Triton is Neptune's largest moon and the only large moon in the Solar System that orbits backward (retrograde).
  • It is almost certainly a captured Kuiper Belt object, snared by Neptune's gravity billions of years ago.
  • Triton's surface is one of the coldest in the Solar System (−235 °C), covered in frozen nitrogen and water ice.
  • Geysers of nitrogen gas erupt from its surface, despite being far from the Sun.
  • Triton is slowly spiraling inward and will eventually be torn apart by Neptune's gravity or crash into the planet.

Nereid

Diameter: 340 km Orbit period: 360 days
  • Nereid has one of the most eccentric orbits of any moon — ranging from 1.4 to 9.7 million km from Neptune.
  • Its irregular orbit suggests it may be a captured Kuiper Belt object, like Triton.
  • It was discovered in 1949 by Gerard Kuiper and is Neptune's third-largest moon.

Moon System

Neptune has 16 confirmed moons. Triton dominates the system; the remaining 13 are small inner moons discovered by Voyager 2 and later ground-based surveys.
