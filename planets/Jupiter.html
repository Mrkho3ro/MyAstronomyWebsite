Jupiter

King of planets — a storm-wracked gas giant with a retinue of moons.

Jupiter is the largest planet in the Solar System — more massive than all other planets combined. This swirling ball of hydrogen and helium has no solid surface; instead, pressure and temperature increase steadily toward a dense core. Its Great Red Spot, banded clouds, and powerful magnetic field make Jupiter one of the most dynamic worlds we study.

Key Facts

  • The Great Red Spot is an anticyclonic storm larger than Earth that has persisted for at least 350 years.
  • Jupiter's rapid rotation flattens it into an oblate spheroid — noticeably wider at the equator.
  • It emits more heat than it receives from the Sun, powered by slow gravitational contraction.
  • Jupiter's magnetic field is the strongest of any planet — 14 times stronger than Earth's.
  • Its gravity acts as a "cosmic vacuum cleaner," deflecting or capturing comets and asteroids.
  • Galileo discovered four large moons in 1610, proving not everything orbits Earth.
  • Jupiter has faint rings discovered by Voyager 1 in 1979, made of dust from its inner moons.
  • The Juno spacecraft (since 2016) has mapped Jupiter's interior, auroras, and polar cyclones.
  • Lightning on Jupiter is three times more powerful than on Earth and occurs in water-cloud layers.
  • A probe entering Jupiter would be crushed and vaporized long before reaching any solid core.

Major Moons — The Galilean Satellites

Io

Diameter: 3,643 km Orbit period: 1.77 days
  • Io is the most volcanically active body in the Solar System, with hundreds of active volcanoes.
  • Jupiter's gravity and tidal forces from Europa and Ganymede heat Io's interior, melting rock.
  • Its surface is covered in sulfur and sulfur dioxide frost, giving it a colorful, mottled appearance.
  • Io's volcanic plumes shoot material hundreds of kilometers into space.

Europa

Diameter: 3,122 km Orbit period: 3.55 days
  • Europa has a smooth, icy crust that may hide a global ocean with more water than all Earth's oceans.
  • Tidal heating from Jupiter keeps the subsurface ocean liquid despite frigid surface temperatures.
  • It is a top target in the search for extraterrestrial life — NASA's Europa Clipper launches in 2024.
  • Reddish cracks and ridges on the surface suggest material welling up from the ocean below.

Ganymede

Diameter: 5,268 km Orbit period: 7.15 days
  • Ganymede is the largest moon in the Solar System — bigger than Mercury and Pluto.
  • It is the only moon known to generate its own magnetic field.
  • Evidence suggests a subsurface saltwater ocean beneath its icy crust.
  • Its surface shows both ancient dark terrain and younger, grooved bright regions.

Callisto

Diameter: 4,821 km Orbit period: 16.69 days
  • Callisto is the most heavily cratered object in the Solar System — its surface is ancient and unchanged for billions of years.
  • It may have a subsurface ocean, but receives less tidal heating than the inner Galilean moons.
  • Callisto's dark surface is covered in ice mixed with rocky material from impacts.
  • It orbits far enough from Jupiter that it experiences less radiation than Io, Europa, or Ganymede.

Amalthea

Diameter: 167 km Orbit period: 0.50 days Distance: 181,400 km
  • Amalthea is Jupiter's fifth-largest moon and the reddest object in the Solar System.
  • Its irregular shape and low density suggest it is a rubble-pile held together by gravity.
  • It orbits inside Jupiter's faint main ring and supplies material to the ring system.
  • Discovered by Edward Barnard in 1892 — the last planetary moon found by direct observation.

Himalia

Diameter: 170 km Orbit period: 250.6 days Distance: 11.5 million km
  • Himalia is the largest of Jupiter's irregular outer moons, likely a captured asteroid.
  • It belongs to the Himalia group — a cluster of small moons sharing similar orbits.
  • Its dark, reddish surface resembles D-type asteroids from the outer Solar System.
  • Named after the nymph Himalia in Greek mythology, who nursed Zeus (Jupiter).
