The Sun

The Sun is the star at the center of our Solar System—a G-type main-sequence star that provides the light and heat that make life on Earth possible. Its gravity holds the planets, moons, asteroids, and comets in orbit.

  • Type: G-type main-sequence star (yellow dwarf)
  • Distance from Earth: ~150 million km (1 AU)
  • Surface temperature: ~5,500 °C (photosphere)
  • Core temperature: ~15 million °C
  • Mass: 99.86% of the Solar System's total mass

Interesting Facts

  • Light from the Sun reaches Earth in about 8 minutes and 20 seconds.
  • The Sun converts about 600 million tons of hydrogen into helium every second through fusion.
  • Solar activity—including sunspots and solar flares—follows an roughly 11-year cycle.
  • The Sun is about 4.6 billion years old and is expected to shine for another 5 billion years.
  • During a total solar eclipse, the Moon briefly blocks the bright photosphere, revealing the corona.
