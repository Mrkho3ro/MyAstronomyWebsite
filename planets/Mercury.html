Mercury

The swift, scorched inner world — closest planet to the Sun.

Mercury is the smallest planet in the Solar System and the closest to the Sun. Its heavily cratered surface resembles Earth's Moon, but extreme temperature swings make it one of the most hostile worlds we know. With almost no atmosphere to retain heat, Mercury's nights are bitterly cold while its days are hot enough to melt lead.

Key Facts

  • Mercury has the most eccentric orbit of any planet, ranging from 46 to 70 million km from the Sun.
  • Despite being closest to the Sun, Venus is hotter because Mercury lacks a thick greenhouse atmosphere.
  • Its iron core makes up about 75% of the planet's radius — one of the largest core-to-body ratios.
  • A 3:2 spin-orbit resonance means Mercury rotates three times for every two orbits around the Sun.
  • Surface features include the Caloris Basin, one of the largest impact basins in the Solar System.
  • Permanent shadowed craters at the poles may harbor water ice despite scorching equatorial heat.
  • Mercury has a weak magnetic field, suggesting at least a partially molten core.
  • It is the second densest planet after Earth, packed with metal and rock.
  • NASA's MESSENGER orbiter (2011–2015) mapped the entire surface in unprecedented detail.
  • From Earth, Mercury is always seen near the horizon at dawn or dusk — never high in a dark sky.
