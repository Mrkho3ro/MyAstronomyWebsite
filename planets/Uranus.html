Uranus

The sideways ice giant — pale blue, cold, and full of surprises.

Ice giant

At a Glance

  • Diameter50,724 km (equator)
  • Mass8.68 × 10²⁵ kg (14.5× Earth)
  • Surface gravity8.87 m/s² (0.91× Earth)*
  • Day length17 h 14 min (retrograde)
  • Year length84 Earth years
  • Atmosphere83% H₂, 15% He, 2% CH₄
  • Avg. cloud temp.~−195 °C
  • Known moons28 confirmed
  • Discovered1781 by William Herschel

Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun and the first discovered with a telescope. This ice giant rotates on its side — its axis is tilted 98° — so it essentially rolls around the Sun. Methane in its upper atmosphere absorbs red light and gives Uranus its distinctive cyan-blue color. It has a faint ring system and a family of icy moons named after characters from Shakespeare and Alexander Pope.

Key Facts

  • Uranus was the first planet discovered in modern times — William Herschel found it in 1781.
  • Its extreme 98° axial tilt may result from a massive collision with an Earth-sized protoplanet.
  • Each pole experiences 42 years of continuous sunlight followed by 42 years of darkness.
  • Uranus has the coldest planetary atmosphere in the Solar System, reaching −224 °C.
  • Unlike Jupiter and Saturn, Uranus emits very little internal heat — its origin is a mystery.
  • It has 13 faint, dark rings discovered in 1977 when they blocked light from a star.
  • Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft to visit Uranus, flying by in January 1986.
  • The planet's magnetic field is tilted 59° from its rotation axis and offset from the center.
  • Uranus and Neptune are classified as "ice giants" — rich in water, ammonia, and methane ices.
  • At its distance, Uranus receives only about 1/400 the sunlight that Earth does.

Major Moons

Miranda

Diameter: 472 km Orbit period: 1.41 days
  • Miranda has the most varied terrain of any moon — cliffs, ridges, and chevron-shaped features.
  • Verona Rupes may be the tallest cliff in the Solar System, dropping 20 km.
  • It may have been shattered and reassembled multiple times by tidal forces.

Ariel

Diameter: 1,158 km Orbit period: 2.52 days
  • Ariel is the brightest of Uranus's major moons, with a young, resurfaced terrain.
  • Its surface shows fault valleys and flows that may have been shaped by icy volcanism.
  • Named after a spirit in Shakespeare's The Tempest.

Umbriel

Diameter: 1,169 km Orbit period: 4.14 days
  • Umbriel is the darkest of Uranus's five major moons, heavily cratered and ancient.
  • Wunda crater has a bright ring of unknown material on its floor.
  • It likely formed from the same disk of debris that created the other major Uranian moons.

Titania

Diameter: 1,578 km Orbit period: 8.71 days
  • Titania is Uranus's largest moon, about half the size of Earth's Moon.
  • Its surface shows deep canyons, fault valleys, and evidence of past geological activity.
  • A possible subsurface ocean may exist, warmed by tidal forces or radioactive decay.
  • Named after the Queen of the Fairies in Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream.

Oberon

Diameter: 1,523 km Orbit period: 13.46 days
  • Oberon is Uranus's second-largest moon and the outermost of the five major satellites.
  • Its ancient, heavily cratered surface suggests little geological activity for billions of years.
  • Large craters on Oberon have bright rays, indicating relatively recent impacts.
  • Named after the King of the Fairies in Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream.

Moon System
