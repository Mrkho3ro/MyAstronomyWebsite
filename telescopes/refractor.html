Refractor Telescopes

Refractors use lenses to bend light to a focus. They produce crisp, high-contrast views — excellent for lunar and planetary detail, double stars, and portable grab-and-go setups.

Celestron Travel Scope 70 DX

Weighing just a few pounds, this achromatic refractor fits in a backpack with its custom carrying case. Set it up in minutes for lunar craters, Jupiter's moons, or the rings of Saturn on vacation — proof that you do not need a large observatory to enjoy the night sky.

Sky-Watcher Evostar 72ED

Extra-low dispersion (ED) glass reduces the color fringing that plagues cheaper refractors, making this doublet a popular first "serious" imaging scope. At 72 mm it captures wide swaths of the Milky Way and large nebulae when paired with a DSLR or dedicated astronomy camera.

72 mm f/6 | ED doublet | ~$450

Celestron NexStar 102SLT

This computerized refractor automatically slews to thousands of objects in its built-in database. The 102 mm aperture delivers noticeably sharper planetary detail than smaller starter scopes, while the GoTo mount removes the frustration of finding faint Messier targets by hand.

102 mm f/6.5 | GoTo alt-az | ~$500

Orion ED80T CF Triplet

A premium apochromatic triplet with carbon-fiber tube, the ED80T delivers exceptional color correction for high-resolution planetary imaging and detailed lunar photography. Three lens elements bring red, green, and blue light to the same focus — the gold standard for refractor optical quality.
