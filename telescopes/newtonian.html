Newtonian / Reflector Telescopes

Newtonian reflectors use a parabolic primary mirror to gather light, with a flat secondary mirror directing the image to a side-mounted eyepiece. They are versatile, affordable, and available on many mount types.

🔭 Dobsonian 🪞 Newtonian 🔍 Refractor ⚙️ Schmidt-Cassegrain 📡 Radio 🌙 Maksutov-Cassegrain

Celestron AstroMaster 130EQ

One of the most recommended first telescopes, the AstroMaster 130EQ pairs a 130 mm Newtonian reflector with a German equatorial mount. Once polar-aligned, the mount lets you track the Moon and planets with a single slow-motion knob — a gentle introduction to how professional telescopes follow the sky.

130 mm f/5 | Equatorial mount | ~$280

Celestron PowerSeeker 114EQ

The PowerSeeker line offers an affordable entry into equatorial-mounted reflectors. Its 114 mm aperture and longer f/8 focal ratio produce sharper planetary views than faster scopes, while still showing bright nebulae and star clusters. A practical choice for students learning both observing and basic mount operation.

114 mm f/8 | Equatorial mount | ~$200

Orion SkyLine 8" f/5 Dobsonian

This fast f/5 Newtonian on a Dobsonian rocker box prioritizes wide-field views of extended nebulae and large star clusters. Without the complexity of an equatorial mount, you simply push the tube to your target — making it a favorite for relaxed deep-sky sessions under dark skies.

203 mm f/5 | Dobsonian mount | ~$450

Sky-Watcher Quattro 250P

Built for astrophotography, the Quattro 250P uses a parabolic primary with a four-vane spider and a coma corrector-ready design. Its fast f/4 focal ratio captures faint nebulae and galaxies in minutes rather than hours — a imaging Newtonian for amateurs stepping beyond visual observing.

254 mm f/4 | Astrophotography Newtonian | ~$900
