Schmidt-Cassegrain / Catadioptric

Schmidt-Cassegrain telescopes (SCTs) combine mirrors and a correcting lens plate in a compact tube. They offer long focal lengths in a portable package — great for planets, smaller deep-sky objects, and GoTo setups.

Celestron NexStar 6SE

The iconic orange-tube Schmidt-Cassegrain folds a 1,500 mm focal length into a compact 6-inch package. Celestron's NexStar GoTo system can locate and track planets, the Moon, and hundreds of deep-sky objects — making it one of the most popular all-around scopes for suburban observers.

150 mm f/10 | GoTo fork mount | ~$900

Celestron NexStar 8SE

Upgrading to 8 inches adds enough light-gathering power to resolve individual stars in globular clusters and reveal the Cassini Division in Saturn's rings. The tube remains remarkably portable for its aperture, which is why the 8SE is a common choice for observers who want one scope for both planets and deep-sky targets.

203 mm f/10 | GoTo fork mount | ~$1,200

Meade LX200 10" ACF

Meade's Advanced Coma-Free optics sharpen stars across the entire field — important for both visual observing and long-exposure imaging. The 10-inch LX200 is a research-capable instrument used by advanced amateurs and university programs alike, with precision GoTo pointing and autoguiding support.

254 mm f/10 | GoTo fork mount | ~$2,500

Learn more ↗

Celestron 114 LCM Computerized

A budget-friendly entry into computerized catadioptric observing, the 114 LCM uses a Maksutov-Newtonian optical design in a compact tube. Its motorized mount offers guided tours of the sky — a helpful teaching tool for beginners who want automatic object location without a large investment.

114 mm f/9 | Maksutov-Newtonian hybrid | ~$350
