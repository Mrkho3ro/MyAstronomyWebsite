Radio Telescopes

Radio telescopes detect radio waves from space — revealing pulsars, hydrogen clouds, active galactic nuclei, and the cosmic microwave background. They range from backyard hydrogen-line dishes to massive interferometer arrays.

🔭 Dobsonian 🪞 Newtonian 🔍 Refractor ⚙️ Schmidt-Cassegrain 📡 Radio 🌙 Maksutov-Cassegrain

Itty Bitty Telescope (IBT)

The IBT is an educational kit that lets students build a working radio telescope tuned to the 21 cm hydrogen line. Point the dish at the Milky Way and you can detect the faint hiss of neutral hydrogen gas — the same signal that revealed the structure of our galaxy to early radio astronomers in the 1930s.

~1 m dish | 21 cm hydrogen line | Educational kit

RTL-SDR + Dish Setup

Hobbyist radio astronomy has been revolutionized by inexpensive software-defined radio (SDR) dongles paired with repurposed satellite TV dishes. For a few hundred dollars, you can detect the Sun, map the Milky Way's hydrogen emission, and even pick up signals from Jupiter's storms.

Software-defined radio | 1–2 m offset dish | ~$200+

Green Bank 20 Meter

At the Green Bank Observatory in West Virginia, the 20-meter telescope serves as a hands-on research instrument for students and educators. Remote observing programs let classrooms propose projects and collect real data on pulsars, star-forming regions, and other radio sources.

20 m dish | L-band / S-band | Research grade

Very Large Array (VLA)

Twenty-seven 25-meter dishes spread across the New Mexico desert work together as one giant interferometer. By combining signals with precise timing, the VLA achieves the resolution of a single dish 36 km wide — revealing structures in galaxies and nebulae invisible to optical telescopes.

27 × 25 m dishes | Interferometer | NM, USA

Learn more ↗

FAST (Five-hundred-meter Aperture)

China's Five-hundred-meter Aperture Spherical Telescope is the world's largest single-dish radio telescope, nestled in a natural karst depression in Guizhou province. FAST detects extraordinarily faint signals — discovering hundreds of pulsars and searching for technosignatures from extraterrestrial civilizations.

500 m dish | World's largest single dish | China
