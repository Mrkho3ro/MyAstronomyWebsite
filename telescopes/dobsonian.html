Dobsonian Telescopes

Dobsonian telescopes use a simple Newtonian reflector on a sturdy alt-azimuth rocker box mount. They deliver the most aperture per dollar — ideal for deep-sky observing of galaxies, nebulae, and star clusters.

Sky-Watcher Heritage 130 Tabletop

This collapsible truss Dobsonian packs a 130 mm mirror into a tabletop package you can carry to a dark-sky site in one trip. Its fast f/5 focal ratio delivers wide, bright views of nebulae and star clusters — ideal for working through the Messier catalog on your first serious observing nights.

Apertura AD8 / Zhumell Z8

The 8-inch Dobsonian is the sweet spot many experienced amateurs recommend: enough aperture to resolve globular clusters and pull detail from galaxies, yet still manageable for one person to move. The AD8 and Z8 are nearly identical full-size rocker-box scopes that have become community favorites for visual deep-sky work.

Celestron StarSense Explorer 8" Dob

Celestron pairs a traditional 8-inch Dobsonian with smartphone-assisted pointing — you align the phone once, then the app guides you to targets without a motorized mount. It keeps the simplicity and light grasp of a Dob while lowering the learning curve for beginners who struggle with star-hopping.

Orion SkyQuest XT10 Classic

Stepping up to 10 inches of aperture reveals fainter galaxies and finer nebula structure that smaller scopes simply cannot reach. The XT10 Classic is a proven full-size Dobsonian with smooth motions and a solid base — a long-term instrument for observers who have outgrown starter scopes and want the deep-sky views a large mirror provides.
